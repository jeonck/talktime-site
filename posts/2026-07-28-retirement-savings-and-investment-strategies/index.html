2026-07-28 Retirement Savings and Investment Strategies

July 28, 2026 · 7 min · 1336 words · jeonck

Session Overview#

The class discussed how the US retirement system works, comparing social security, pensions, and 401(k) accounts. Learners also explored investment strategies including index funds, ETFs, and individual stocks, and discussed why most individual investors tend to underperform the broader market. The session ended with a conversation about compound interest, long-term holding strategies, and the emotional challenges of investing.

💬 Idioms#

“bump up”#

a sudden or noticeable increase in an amount or level

  • If you delay claiming your retirement benefit until age seventy, you receive a significant bump up in your monthly payment.
  • After three years at the same company, she finally received a bump up in her salary.

“get a head start”#

to begin something earlier than others, gaining an early advantage

  • Starting to save money in your twenties gives you a real head start on retirement.
  • He arrived an hour early to get a head start on the day’s work.

“on a roll”#

experiencing a continuous streak of success or good luck

  • After winning three hands in a row at the card table, he felt like he was on a roll.
  • The company was on a roll, releasing two hit products in the same quarter.

“diamond in the rough”#

a person or thing with great hidden potential that has not yet been fully developed or recognized

  • The coach saw the newcomer as a diamond in the rough and spent extra time training her.
  • That overlooked stock turned out to be a diamond in the rough — its value quadrupled within three years.

📚 Vocabulary to Remember#

  • pension — a fixed regular payment made to a retired person by a former employer, guaranteed for life
    • After thirty years of public service, he retired with a generous pension that covered all his basic expenses.
  • 401(k) — a US employer-sponsored retirement savings account where the employee chooses how much to contribute and how to invest the money
    • She increased her monthly contribution to her 401(k) so she would have a larger balance when she eventually retired.
  • compound interest — interest calculated on both the original amount and the interest already earned, causing savings to grow faster over time
    • Thanks to compound interest, the small deposit she made at age twenty had grown enormously by the time she reached retirement.
  • contribute — to regularly put money into a savings or investment account
    • He set up an automatic transfer to contribute a fixed amount to his retirement fund every month.
  • tax-advantaged — describes an account or investment that offers special government tax benefits, such as reduced taxes on earnings
    • Enrolling in a tax-advantaged savings plan can help your money grow faster because less of it goes to the government each year.
  • diversify — to spread investments across different types of assets in order to reduce risk
    • Rather than putting all her savings into one company, she chose to diversify across several industries.
  • liquidate — to sell an investment and convert it into cash
    • He had to liquidate part of his portfolio to pay for an unexpected expense.
  • index fund — an investment fund designed to track the performance of a broad market index, offering wide exposure at low cost
    • Instead of picking individual stocks, she put her savings into an index fund and did not touch it for fifteen years.

🔧 Say It Better#

  1. ❌ Since I only were 16 years, I have a whole bunch of zeros. ✅ Since I only worked for 16 years, I have a whole bunch of zeros. 💡 Use the past verb ‘worked’ plus ‘for’ to describe a duration of employment; ‘were’ is a form of ‘be,’ not ‘work.’
  2. ❌ 35 is very long period, right? ✅ 35 years is a very long period, right? 💡 State the unit (‘years’) and add the article ‘a’ before a singular countable noun such as ‘period.’
  3. ❌ I must continue with the working. ✅ I must continue working. 💡 After ‘continue,’ use a gerund (verb + -ing) directly; ‘with the’ is not needed here.
  4. ❌ Only one stock is fail, I think. ✅ Only one stock has failed, I think. 💡 Use the present perfect (‘has failed’) to describe a past event whose result still matters now.
  5. ❌ Increasing is long time. Put down quickly. ✅ It takes a long time to go up, but it can drop very quickly. 💡 Each clause needs a clear subject (‘it’) and a complete verb phrase; avoid omitting both.
  6. ❌ Cannot endure the price down. ✅ I can’t endure the price going down. 💡 Include the subject ‘I’; after ’endure,’ use a gerund phrase (’the price going down’), not an adjective alone.
  7. ❌ My invest resort is minus 10% lost. ✅ My investment result is a 10% loss. 💡 ‘Investment’ is the noun form of ‘invest’; the correct word is ‘result,’ not ‘resort’; say ‘a 10% loss’ rather than ‘minus X% lost.’
  8. ❌ It depends on person. ✅ It depends on the person. 💡 The expression ‘depend on’ requires ’the’ before a singular countable noun used in a general sense.
  9. ❌ I have worked for the IT field. ✅ I have worked in the IT field. 💡 Use ‘in’ for a field or industry; ‘for’ is reserved for a specific employer or organization.

✅ Check Yourself#

Q1. She decided to ____ her portfolio by putting money into stocks, bonds, and real estate instead of just one company.

  • liquidate
  • diversify
  • pension
Show answer

diversify — 'Diversify' means to spread investments across different assets to reduce risk; 'liquidate' means to sell everything, and a 'pension' is a type of retirement payment — neither describes spreading investments.

Q2. His small deposit kept growing year after year because of ____ — the interest he earned began earning interest of its own.

  • compound interest
  • pension
  • index fund
Show answer

compound interest — 'Compound interest' describes the mechanism where returns earn further returns; a 'pension' and an 'index fund' are financial products, not growth mechanisms.

Q3. The sales team had closed a new deal every single day that week; they were clearly ____.

  • on a roll
  • diamond in the rough
  • get a head start
Show answer

on a roll — 'On a roll' describes a run of consecutive successes; 'diamond in the rough' refers to unrecognized potential, and 'get a head start' means to begin early — neither fits a winning streak.

Q4. By opening a retirement account right after graduation, she managed to ____ over classmates who waited until their thirties.

  • bump up
  • on a roll
  • get a head start
Show answer

get a head start — 'Get a head start' means to gain an early advantage; 'bump up' is a sudden increase in amount, and 'on a roll' describes ongoing success — neither captures starting earlier than others.

Q5. When the market crashed, he was forced to ____ his savings at a loss just to pay his monthly bills.

  • contribute
  • liquidate
  • compound interest
Show answer

liquidate — 'Liquidate' means to sell investments and turn them into cash; 'contribute' means to add money in, and 'compound interest' is a growth process — neither means selling off assets.

Q6. Enrolling in a ____ savings plan means a portion of your investment gains is sheltered from taxes each year.

  • tax-advantaged
  • compound interest
  • index fund
Show answer

tax-advantaged — 'Tax-advantaged' describes the special tax treatment of an account; 'compound interest' is a growth mechanism, and 'index fund' is a type of investment product — neither describes a tax benefit.

✍️ Mini Diary#

After dinner tonight, I finally sat down to review my retirement savings — something I had been putting off for weeks. I was relieved to see that compound interest had quietly done its work: my balance had grown more than I expected simply by leaving it alone. I reminded myself how glad I was that I had chosen a tax-advantaged account years ago and decided to contribute a small fixed amount every month, even during tighter times. It felt good to know I had gotten a real head start on my future, instead of waiting until the pressure felt urgent.
