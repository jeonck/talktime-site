2026-08-01 AI Tools, Privacy, and the Art of Learning a Language

August 1, 2026 · 7 min · 1339 words · jeonck

Session Overview#

The session centered on a custom transcription tool one participant built to record class conversations and turn them into vocabulary lists and study notes on a public website. The group discussed whether sharing personal conversations with cloud-based AI services raised privacy and confidentiality concerns, and how consent should work in that setting. The conversation then broadened into a rich exploration of how people best learn languages — weighing technology and speed against immersion, real-life relationships, and lived experience.

💬 Idioms#

“weigh the pros and cons”#

to carefully think through the advantages and disadvantages of something before making a decision

  • Before joining any language-exchange app, it is worth taking time to weigh the pros and cons.
  • The group weighed the pros and cons of sharing their class recordings before making a decision together.

“at the click of a finger”#

instantly or with almost no effort; often used to describe how quickly technology can do something

  • With today’s AI tools, you can compile a list of phrasal verbs at the click of a finger.
  • Information that once took days to find in a library is now available at the click of a finger.

“live the language”#

to learn a language through real, everyday experiences and human relationships rather than just textbooks

  • Moving abroad and building friendships helped her truly live the language.
  • You can study every grammar rule in the book, but you really have to live the language to sound natural.

“mother tongue”#

the first language a person learned as a child; one’s native language

  • When he gets emotional in an argument, he always switches back to his mother tongue.
  • She finds it easier to express complicated feelings in her mother tongue than in a second language.

📚 Vocabulary to Remember#

  • transcribe — to convert spoken words into written text
    • The app automatically transcribes everything said in the meeting into a readable text file.
  • consent — permission that someone gives freely and with full understanding
    • Before recording the session, the organizer asked every participant for their consent.
  • confidentiality — the practice of keeping personal or sensitive information private and not sharing it without permission
    • The platform has strict confidentiality rules to protect the data its users share.
  • nuance — a subtle difference in meaning, feeling, or tone that is not immediately obvious
    • Even after years of study, grasping every nuance of a second language takes real-world practice.
  • colloquial — belonging to informal, everyday spoken language rather than formal or written language
    • The phrase ‘gonna’ is colloquial; in formal writing you would use ‘going to’.
  • precaution — a careful action taken in advance to prevent a problem or reduce a risk
    • As a precaution, the developer removed all names from the transcript before posting it online.
  • immersion — surrounding yourself completely in a language or culture in order to learn it more naturally and quickly
    • Total immersion in a new environment is one of the fastest ways to pick up a language.
  • prompt engineering — the skill of writing clear, well-structured instructions to get the best results from an AI system
    • Good prompt engineering can turn a vague AI response into something detailed and truly useful.

🔧 Say It Better#

  1. ❌ There is many mistakes from me. ✅ I make many mistakes. 💡 Use ’there are’ with a plural noun (’there are many mistakes’); the simpler and more natural rewrite is ‘I make many mistakes’.
  2. ❌ If don’t use this tool, then I will change it in privately. ✅ If we don’t use this tool, I will change it privately. 💡 A subject is required before the verb (‘we don’t’); ‘in privately’ is incorrect — the adverb ‘privately’ stands alone with no preposition.
  3. ❌ I never thinking I can use it. ✅ I never thought I could use it. 💡 Use simple past (’thought’) and the past modal (‘could’) when describing what you believed at an earlier time.
  4. ❌ My husband don’t have time to bring me home. ✅ My husband doesn’t have time to take me. 💡 Third-person singular requires ‘doesn’t’; use ’take’ when moving someone away from the speaker, and ‘bring’ when moving toward the speaker.
  5. ❌ This application will develop by yourself. ✅ Did you develop this application yourself? 💡 The intended meaning is a question; mixing ‘will’ with a passive structure creates confusion — rephrase as a direct question in the simple past.
  6. ❌ I didn’t more effort to this. ✅ I didn’t put much effort into this. 💡 The natural collocation is ‘put effort into something’; the verb ‘put’ and the preposition ‘into’ are both required.
  7. ❌ I think learning language is not easy to quickly. ✅ I think learning a language too quickly is not easy. 💡 Add the article ‘a’ before a singular countable noun (‘a language’); move ’too quickly’ directly after what it modifies.
  8. ❌ Cannot easily denuance. ✅ You cannot easily grasp the nuance. 💡 ‘Denuance’ is not a real word; the correct noun is ’nuance’, and the natural phrase is ‘grasp the nuance’ or ‘pick up on the nuance’.
  9. ❌ I have a big disadvantage to compare English and Spanish. ✅ I have a big disadvantage when comparing English and Spanish. 💡 Use ‘when + -ing’ to describe a situation or circumstance; ’to compare’ sounds like a stated purpose rather than a condition.
  10. ❌ I found a podcast that an influence reunite more than 100 phrasal verbs. ✅ I found a podcast where an influencer compiled more than 100 phrasal verbs. 💡 Use ‘where’ for a source or place; the person is an ‘influencer’ (not ‘influence’); ‘compiled’ or ‘gathered’ fits better than ‘reunite’.

  • colloquial

  • prompt engineering

  • at the click of a finger
  • weigh the pros and cons
  • immersion
  • precaution

Q5. She understood each word individually, but the ____ of the joke still escaped her.

  • nuance
  • precaution
  • colloquial
  • consent
Show answer

nuance — 'Nuance' means a subtle difference in meaning or tone; 'colloquial' describes an informal language style, not a quality of meaning.

Q6. She took every ____ to make sure no personal details appeared on the published website.

  • precaution
  • consent
  • immersion
  • nuance

precaution — 'Take a precaution' is a natural collocation meaning to act carefully in advance to avoid a problem; 'consent' means giving permission, which doesn't fit the blank here.

✍️ Mini Diary#

Today I had a lot to think about after class. A classmate showed us a tool they had built that can transcribe our conversations and turn them into a study website — almost at the click of a finger. It sounded impressive, but I found myself quietly weighing the pros and cons: the vocabulary notes would be wonderful for review, yet some of what we share in class feels personal. In the end, we agreed to try it for just one session, and I left feeling that to really improve, you have to live the language — messy, human moments and all.
