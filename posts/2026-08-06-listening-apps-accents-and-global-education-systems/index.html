2026-08-06 Listening Apps, Accents, and Global Education Systems

August 6, 2026 · 7 min · 1369 words · jeonck

Session Overview#

The session opened with a participant demonstrating a self-built browser-based dictation app that reads sentences aloud and scores how accurately listeners can transcribe them, leading to a discussion of pronunciation tools and speech-recognition services. Conversation then shifted to regional accents — including Southern American and British varieties — and the challenge of tonal languages such as Mandarin, Cantonese, and Vietnamese. The group wrapped up by comparing tutoring cultures, school discipline practices, and university pathways across several countries, sharing personal stories of childhood independence and long language-learning journeys.

💬 Idioms#

“pick (something) up on the way out”#

to collect or take care of something as you leave a place, without making a special extra trip

  • She said she’d pick up the printed notes on her way out of the classroom.
  • I’ll grab the package and pick it up on my way out of the building.

“word by word”#

one word at a time, very slowly and carefully, without natural fluency

  • Before translation apps existed, he had to decode every foreign menu word by word.
  • She read the legal contract word by word to make sure she understood every clause.

“make a difference”#

to have a noticeable or important effect on something

  • Practising dictation every day really makes a difference to your listening accuracy.
  • Living in a country where the language is spoken makes a huge difference to your fluency.

“on purpose”#

done deliberately and intentionally, not by accident

  • The teacher made the listening sentences confusing on purpose to challenge the class.
  • He used a different accent on purpose to test whether the speech app would still transcribe him correctly.

📚 Vocabulary to Remember#

  • dictation — a listening exercise in which you hear spoken words and write them down exactly as you hear them
    • The dictation exercise revealed that she was mishearing certain vowel sounds.
  • pronunciation — the way a word or language is spoken; how sounds are produced and combined
    • The app let him record himself and compare his pronunciation with a model speaker.
  • accuracy — how correct or error-free something is; often expressed as a percentage of right answers
    • Her accuracy on the listening test was below sixty percent because the sentences were deliberately tricky.
  • accent — a distinctive way of pronouncing a language, usually linked to a particular region or background
    • After spending time abroad, she noticed her accent had gradually begun to shift.
  • tongue twister — a phrase or sentence that is very difficult to say quickly because it contains similar or repeated sounds
    • The teacher used a tongue twister to help students practise the difference between two sounds they kept confusing.
  • tone — in linguistics, the pitch or musical quality on a syllable that changes its meaning — central to languages such as Mandarin, Cantonese, and Vietnamese
    • In Mandarin, the same syllable can mean ‘mother’, ‘hemp’, or ‘horse’ depending on its tone.
  • tutoring — extra private teaching given outside regular school hours, usually one-on-one or in a small group
    • Many families pay for after-school tutoring to help their children prepare for entrance exams.
  • connecting words — words or phrases (also called linking words or discourse markers) that join ideas and help speech or writing flow naturally, such as ‘however’, ’therefore’, or ’even though’
    • Learning connecting words made her spoken answers sound far more organised and natural.

🔧 Say It Better#

  1. ❌ which songs you said is wrong ✅ which words you said were wrong 💡 The subject ‘words’ is plural, so the verb must be ‘were’, not ‘is’; ‘words’ (not ‘songs’) is also the intended meaning here.
  2. ❌ Is there a proof charging? / No free of charging. ✅ Is it free of charge? / No, there is no free charge. 💡 The fixed phrase is ‘free of charge’ — no article and no ‘-ing’ ending on ‘charge’.
  3. ❌ Today I remember. Tomorrow, I will forgot. ✅ Today I remember it, but tomorrow I will forget it. 💡 After a modal verb like ‘will’, always use the base form (‘forget’), not the past tense (‘forgot’).
  4. ❌ You believe me? (used to mean ‘do you have confidence in my ability to do this?’) ✅ Do you trust me? / Do you believe in me? 💡 ‘Believe someone’ means you think they are telling the truth; ’trust someone’ or ‘believe in someone’ expresses confidence in their ability or reliability.
  5. ❌ After I graduate, I throw out the book. ✅ After I graduated, I threw out the book. 💡 ‘Throw’ is irregular; its past tense is ’threw’. Use past tense throughout when telling a finished story.
  6. ❌ You must should do it. ✅ You should do it. 💡 English uses only one modal verb at a time; ‘must’ and ‘should’ cannot be combined.
  7. ❌ Some persons tell me. ✅ Some people told me. 💡 ‘People’ is the normal plural for humans in general conversation; ‘persons’ is very formal. Use past tense ’told’ when narrating past events.
  8. ❌ Why happened? ✅ Why did that happen? 💡 English questions need an auxiliary verb (‘did’) and a subject before the main verb.
  9. ❌ I was didn’t care about it. ✅ I didn’t care about it. 💡 Use either ‘was’ (for the verb ‘be’) or ‘didn’t’ (for a past negative action) — not both together.
  10. ❌ I don’t have so many choice to go to America. ✅ I didn’t have so many choices to go to America. 💡 ‘Choice’ is countable; after ‘so many’, use the plural ‘choices’. The past tense ‘didn’t have’ also fits the story context better.

✅ Check Yourself#

Q1. She recorded herself speaking and played it back to check her ____; she wanted to hear whether her sounds were correct.

  • pronunciation
  • tutoring
  • accent
Show answer

pronunciation — 'Pronunciation' is the specific way sounds are produced when speaking; 'tutoring' is extra teaching and 'accent' is a broader regional speech pattern — neither fits a sound-by-sound self-check.

Q2. The class repeated the difficult phrase over and over — it was a classic ____ designed to train the ear on two sounds that are easy to mix up.

  • tongue twister
  • connecting words
  • dictation
Show answer

tongue twister — A 'tongue twister' is a phrase made hard to say quickly by repeated similar sounds; 'dictation' is a writing exercise and 'connecting words' are linking phrases — neither matches.

Q3. Committing to even ten minutes of focused listening practice every evening really ____.

  • makes a difference
  • on purpose
  • word by word
Show answer

makes a difference — 'Makes a difference' expresses a noticeable positive effect; 'on purpose' means deliberately and 'word by word' describes pace — neither completes the sentence naturally.

Q4. He admitted he had used a strong regional accent ____ to see whether the speech-recognition app would still understand him.

  • on purpose
  • accuracy
  • tone
Show answer

on purpose — 'On purpose' means deliberately or intentionally, which fits perfectly; 'accuracy' is a noun and 'tone' is a linguistic feature — neither works as an adverbial in this blank.

Q5. In Vietnamese, the same syllable can carry six completely different meanings because the language has six distinct ____s.

  • tone
  • accent
  • tutoring
Show answer

tone — A 'tone' in linguistics is the pitch pattern on a syllable that changes a word's meaning; 'accent' is a regional speech style and 'tutoring' is extra teaching — neither fits the linguistic context.

Q6. Without a smartphone, she had to translate every sign ____, looking up each unfamiliar term one at a time in a paper dictionary.

  • word by word
  • connecting words
  • on purpose
Show answer

word by word — 'Word by word' means one word at a time, slowly and carefully; 'connecting words' are linking phrases and 'on purpose' means intentionally — neither describes the pace of translating.
