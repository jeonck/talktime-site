2026-07-30 Shopping Habits, Southern Culture, and American History

July 30, 2026 · 7 min · 1405 words · jeonck

Session Overview#

The class began with tips for visiting a large university campus, then shifted into American history through a discussion of LBJ’s presidency and the Voting Rights Act of the 1960s. Participants explored the concept of southern hospitality and how it contrasts with the busier pace of life in large northern cities, briefly touching on the novel ‘To Kill a Mockingbird’ as a cultural reference. The session closed with a lively comparison of online shopping platforms across several countries and a discussion of the advantages of visiting local farmer’s markets and independent shops in person.

💬 Idioms#

“southern hospitality”#

The tradition of exceptional warmth, friendliness, and generous welcome associated with people from the American South.

  • We felt real southern hospitality the moment we arrived — our hosts had cooked a full meal and stayed up talking with us until midnight.
  • The small inn had a reputation for southern hospitality; every guest was greeted by name and offered homemade biscuits in the morning.

“a gift for”#

A natural talent or special ability for a particular skill or activity.

  • She has a gift for languages and was already conversational in Korean after only six months of study.
  • My grandfather had a gift for storytelling — he could turn a five-minute errand into an hour-long adventure.

“pros and cons”#

The advantages (pros) and disadvantages (cons) of a choice or situation, weighed together.

  • We thought carefully about the pros and cons of ordering groceries online versus shopping at the market in person.
  • There are pros and cons to living in a big city: plenty of opportunities, but also noise, traffic, and high prices.

“slow-paced”#

Describes a lifestyle or environment that is relaxed, unhurried, and free from constant pressure.

  • After ten years in a crowded city, she was ready for the slow-paced life of a small town.
  • The documentary showed a slow-paced village where neighbors still gathered on porches every evening just to talk.

📚 Vocabulary to Remember#

  • contemporary — A person who lived or worked during the same period as another; belonging to the same era.
    • The civil rights leader and the president were contemporaries who sometimes worked toward the same goals.
  • discriminate — To treat a person or group unfairly based on characteristics such as race, religion, or gender.
    • The new law made it illegal to discriminate against any citizen when it came to the right to vote.
  • notorious — Widely known for something negative or harmful.
    • That stretch of highway was notorious for accidents caused by speeding drivers.
  • autonomous — Having the right or power to govern itself independently, without full control from a larger authority.
    • The region has an autonomous status, meaning it manages its own cultural and religious affairs locally.
  • organic — Produced without artificial chemicals, synthetic fertilizers, or pesticides.
    • Many shoppers willingly pay more for organic vegetables because they prefer food grown without chemicals.
  • distribution center — A large warehouse where products are stored and then shipped out to customers.
    • Because a distribution center opened nearby, online orders now arrive by the following morning.
  • obey — To follow a rule, law, or instruction as required.
    • Most drivers try to obey the speed limit, but on some highways many people go much faster.
  • supply chain — The whole network of people, companies, and steps involved in getting a product from its origin to the customer.
    • The global crisis disrupted the supply chain, causing delays and shortages of many everyday goods.

🔧 Say It Better#

  1. ❌ the people is nice ✅ the people are nice 💡 ‘People’ is always plural in English, so the verb must be ‘are’, never ‘is’.
  2. ❌ I have fine several products that are cheaper in other places than Amazon ✅ I have found several products that are cheaper in other places than on Amazon 💡 The past participle of ‘find’ is ‘found’, not ‘fine’; also use ‘on Amazon’, not ‘in Amazon’.
  3. ❌ The delivered services is expensive ✅ The delivery service is expensive 💡 Use the noun ‘delivery’ (not the adjective ‘delivered’) to modify ‘service’; ‘service’ is singular here, so the verb is ‘is’.
  4. ❌ I not a member of Facebook ✅ I am not a member of Facebook 💡 English negation with ’not’ always requires the verb ’to be’: ‘I am not’, ‘she is not’, ’they are not’.
  5. ❌ I sell one of my car in Craig list ✅ I sold one of my cars on Craigslist 💡 Use past tense ‘sold’; ‘car’ must be plural after ‘one of my’; use the preposition ‘on’ with platform and website names.
  6. ❌ It depends. You want to are very interested in the history of Texas. ✅ It depends. If you are very interested in the history of Texas, it is a great choice. 💡 Use ‘if’ to introduce a condition; ‘you want to are’ is not grammatical — the conditional structure is simply ‘if you are’.
  7. ❌ some of Americans didn’t have right for support ✅ some Americans didn’t have the right to vote 💡 Do not add ‘of’ between ‘some’ and a noun; ‘right’ needs the article ’the’; the correct phrase is ’the right to vote’.
  8. ❌ I didn’t want to be lazy for order online ✅ I didn’t want to be lazy and just order online 💡 Use the conjunction ‘and’ (not the preposition ‘for’) to connect two related actions in one clause.
  9. ❌ Very struggling with the life ✅ People there really struggle with life 💡 ‘Very’ modifies adjectives and adverbs, not verbs; use ‘really struggle’ or ‘struggle a lot’ to add emphasis to a verb.
  10. ❌ I have visited Capitol ✅ I have visited the Capitol 💡 Unique, one-of-a-kind buildings and institutions take the definite article ’the’ — the Capitol, the White House, the Eiffel Tower.

✅ Check Yourself#

Q1. That town was once ____ for the unfair barriers it placed on certain groups of voters in the 1960s.

  • notorious
  • autonomous
  • contemporary
Show answer

notorious — 'Notorious' means widely known for something negative, which fits a place known for harmful voting policies; 'autonomous' means self-governing, and 'contemporary' means from the same time period — neither describes a bad reputation.

Q2. My colleague has ____ public speaking — she kept the entire audience engaged for over an hour.

  • a gift for
  • southern hospitality
  • pros and cons
Show answer

a gift for — 'A gift for' expresses a natural talent for a specific skill; 'southern hospitality' is a cultural tradition of warmth, and 'pros and cons' refers to advantages and disadvantages — neither describes a personal ability.

Q3. We only buy ____ produce at the farmer’s market because we want food grown without pesticides or chemicals.

  • organic
  • slow-paced
  • notorious
Show answer

organic — 'Organic' specifically describes food produced without artificial chemicals; 'slow-paced' describes a relaxed lifestyle, and 'notorious' means known for something bad — neither describes a farming method.

Q4. Before we decide, let’s think through the ____ of shopping online versus going to the store in person.

  • pros and cons
  • supply chain
  • autonomous
Show answer

pros and cons — 'Pros and cons' means the advantages and disadvantages of a choice, which fits a decision-making context; 'supply chain' is a business logistics term, and 'autonomous' means self-governing — neither fits weighing a personal choice.

Q5. Even though the speed limit was clearly posted, many drivers chose not to ____ it.

  • obey
  • discriminate
  • contemporary
Show answer

obey — 'Obey' means to follow a rule or law, which fits perfectly here; 'discriminate' means to treat someone unfairly based on a characteristic, and 'contemporary' is a noun/adjective meaning of the same era — neither fits following a traffic rule.

✍️ Mini Diary#

Today after class I decided to visit a local farmer’s market I had heard about, and from the moment I arrived I could feel the difference from my usual rushed errands. The whole place had a wonderfully slow-paced atmosphere — vendors leaned over their tables, chatted with every customer, and nobody seemed to be in any hurry at all. There was real southern hospitality in the air: one seller offered me a sample of honey even before I asked. I found some beautiful organic vegetables I had never seen before and spent a long time talking to the grower about how she raised them without any chemicals. Walking back to my car with a small jar of honey in hand, I thought about the pros and cons of always ordering online — it is certainly convenient, but I would have missed every bit of this.
